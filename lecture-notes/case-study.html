Running case study · thesis evidence audit

From process performance to cell metabolism

This is a case from your own work, not a claim that the thesis measured intracellular metabolism. Use it throughout the course to practice moving from a process observation to a biological hypothesis, then asking what evidence would distinguish that hypothesis from alternatives.

What the thesis reports

  • Process: semi-continuous cassava retting in a moving-tray reactor, described as 60 kg/day, with eight trays and 2.5 kg of chips fed per tray each hour. The chips were 1–1.5 mm thick. The starter is named as Lactobacillus plantarum, Bacillus subtilis, and Aspergillus oryzae in most passages (8 L of each culture; 24 L total), but one starter-preparation passage says Aspergillus niger (printed p. 46 / PDF p. 70). No strain identifiers or independent identity check are given; treat the fungal identity as unresolved. The broth was circulated; the validation operated at an 8-hour tray residence time.
  • Factors varied: residence time (8 or 12 hours), water-to-cassava loading, and broth-water replacement. The reported selected condition was 8 hours and a 7:1 water-to-substrate ratio. The replacement interval is inconsistent in the thesis: the abstract and methods say 25% at every residence time (8 hours), while the validation procedure says 25% every 4 hours; it was run for five days. Treat the interval as unresolved until the laboratory records are checked.
  • Measurements: counts for L. plantarum and B. subtilis, gravimetric A. oryzae, broth pH, cyanogenic compounds in broth and flour, flour yield, moisture, swelling power, and whiteness. The reported validation averages include 35% flour yield, 13% moisture, 10.4% swelling power, and 83.5% whiteness.

Keep the levels of explanation separate. The thesis reports process outcomes and microbial measurements. Its explanations—such as starch hydrolysis supplying glucose, L. plantarum acidifying the broth, or a consortium member limiting conversion—are hypotheses unless the corresponding substrate, metabolite, enzyme, or flux was directly measured. pH alone does not identify which acids were produced or quantify their rates.

A first evidence audit

The thesis's standard table gives whiteness a minimum of 87 and moisture a maximum of 13%; its validation discussion reports 83.5 whiteness and 13% moisture, while the abstract and conclusion describe the product as meeting the standard. The whiteness result appears below the stated threshold. Treat the compliance claim as unresolved until you check the exact standard scope, the reported metric, and the underlying result. This is a useful example of checking a conclusion against its own criteria.

There is also a ratio-notation issue worth resolving before any calculation. The abstract and conclusion describe water-to-substrate as 7:1. The methods narrative uses the reciprocal substrate-to-water 1:7: that is the same condition if the basis is stated, not a second value. The methods table, however, is headed “Rasio air terhadap substrat” and lists 1:7 and 1:12, so its label and values conflict; Table 4.1's separate Run 2 is 1:8, a different tested condition. The uncertainty is the table labelling and which run is being compared, not the reciprocal arithmetic. Check the experimental records, state kg water per kg fresh cassava, and do not silently normalize the labels.

Checked against the thesis. The process, 8-hour residence time, five-day validation, 35% yield, 13% moisture, 10.4% swelling power and 83.5% whiteness are all in the source. The starter volumes and the two bacterial names are in the source, but the fungal name is not consistent: most passages say A. oryzae, one starter-preparation passage says A. niger (printed p. 46 / PDF p. 70), and there are no strain identifiers. The 25% replacement is stated at every residence time in the abstract and methods, and every 4 hours in the validation procedure (printed p. 103 / PDF p. 127); both are in the source, so neither interval is settled. The whiteness result is below the SNI minimum of 87 in Table 4.8, while the abstract and conclusion say the product meets SNI. These are source-level issues, not transcription errors. See [TK4092] Table 3.4, Table 4.1, Table 4.8, validation pp. 101–109, conclusion p. 111.

What this case can teach across the module

  • Weeks 8–18: draw a carbon and redox map from cassava starch to sugars, biomass, acids, and any other products. Mark every quantity the thesis measured and leave the rest as unknown; then ask what a full carbon balance would require.
  • Weeks 20–29: build a small, explicitly hypothetical network and ask what FBA could predict. The thesis's pH and cell counts do not determine intracellular fluxes. Propose time-resolved sugar, organic-acid, gas, and 13C-labeling measurements that could constrain them.
  • Weeks 32–39: treat the mixed starter as an ecological system. The species-specific counts are useful, but the thesis also reports contamination and limitations in some counting methods. Ask what selective assays, controls, or strain-specific molecular measurements would be needed to distinguish the intended organisms from contaminants.
  • Weeks 47–52: connect tray residence time, circulation, water use, and product recovery to organism physiology. A process optimum need not be a cellular metabolic optimum; ask which process variables changed the cells' environment and which changed only handling or recovery.

Case teach-back

  1. Explain in your own words what the study optimized and what it did not measure about intracellular metabolism.
  2. Calculate the water requirement from the reported loading ratio for a stated cassava mass. First resolve the ratio notation; write units and show the interpretation you chose.
  3. Name one metabolic explanation for the observed pH or product outcome, one competing explanation, and the measurement that would distinguish them.
  4. Audit one conclusion against its data and criterion. Record any mismatch without forcing the evidence to fit the conclusion.

Source within your thesis: Chapters III–V, especially the methods and variation tables, the microbial-growth and pH results, Table 4.8, and the validation discussion (printed pages 43–54 / PDF 67–78, 57–80 / PDF 81–104, 81–109 / PDF 105–133, and 111 / PDF 135). Use the original tables and figures when extracting numerical data; the summary above is an orientation map.
