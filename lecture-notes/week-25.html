Gene deletions, essentiality and phase planes

Week content

The first point at which your model meets experimental truth and loses. Validating against essentiality data and then explaining every disagreement is what separates a modeller from someone who has run a tutorial, and it is the single most valuable artifact you will produce in this phase.

Core ideas

1. How essentiality is predicted, and the threshold hiding inside it

Delete a gene by applying the GPR logic, constrain the affected reactions to zero, re-solve, and compare predicted growth with the wild type. Then apply a threshold: below some fraction of wild-type growth, typically 1% to 10%, the gene is called essential. That threshold is a modelling choice and it matters — a gene whose deletion leaves 5% growth is called essential at a 10% threshold and non-essential at 1%, and experimentally such a strain grows slowly rather than not at all. Report the threshold you used and show the sensitivity to it; many published screens do neither.

2. Validation, and the confusion matrix as a starting point

Compare against experimental data — the Keio single-gene deletion collection for E. coli, or equivalents for other organisms — and build the matrix: true positives are genes both call essential, false positives are predicted essential but experimentally dispensable, false negatives the reverse. Compute precision and recall. Typical genome-scale performance is precision near 0.6 to 0.8 and recall near 0.8 to 0.9, which sounds respectable until you notice that the matrix alone tells you nothing about why. The matrix is where the work starts.

3. False positives, which dominate, and their four causes

Predicted essential, experimentally dispensable. A missing isozyme or an unannotated promiscuous activity provides the reaction by another route. A missing transporter or an unrecorded medium component lets the cell bypass the blocked step by uptake — rich medium is a frequent culprit, since experiments are often done on it and models on minimal. A GPR written as AND where the biology is OR. Or the growth threshold is too strict. Each has a different remedy, and classifying them gene by gene is the actual result of a validation study; a matrix without that classification is worth perhaps a third of the marks.

4. False negatives, which are more interesting

Predicted dispensable, experimentally essential. Regulation the model does not represent, so the alternative route exists stoichiometrically but is not expressed. Accumulation of a toxic intermediate when the normal route is blocked, which stoichiometry cannot see. Moonlighting functions outside metabolism entirely. Or a gene essential for a reason the model is not about — cell division, envelope integrity. These are fewer but they point at the model's structural limits rather than at missing knowledge, which is why they are worth discussing individually in a write-up.

5. Double deletions and synthetic lethality

Run pairs and you find synthetic lethal combinations: neither single deletion is lethal, the double is. These identify redundancy — isozymes, parallel routes, alternative cofactor usage — and they are the model's most genuinely predictive output, because redundancy is hard to see by inspection. The combinatorics is manageable at genome scale for pairs, roughly 10⁶ to 10⁷ simulations, and hopeless for triples. For engineering, synthetic lethal pairs are also a warning list: they are the interventions where a single deletion appears safe and the combination is not.

6. Robustness analysis and phenotypic phase planes

Vary one bound and plot the objective: robustness analysis, which shows where a parameter matters and where it does not, and where the limiting constraint changes. Vary two — typically carbon and oxygen uptake — and you get a phenotypic phase plane: a two-dimensional map divided into regions, each with a distinct pattern of active constraints and by-product secretion, separated by lines where the limiting constraint switches. The line of optimality is the locus of maximum objective, and the empirical finding that organisms often sit near it is one of the field's more striking validations. Explain the regions from stoichiometry before reading them off the plot; the explanation is the learning.

Worked anchor

Read a confusion matrix properly.

A genome-scale screen predicts 318 essential genes; experiment reports 274, with 221 in common.

True positives 221. False positives 318 − 221 = 97. False negatives 274 − 221 = 53. Precision = 221/318 = 0.695. Recall = 221/274 = 0.807. F1 = 0.747.

Now the part that is actually the result. False positives outnumber false negatives roughly two to one, which is the usual pattern and tells you the model is over-predicting essentiality — it knows fewer alternative routes than the cell has. Classify all 97: expect a large group resolved by isozymes or promiscuous activities, a group explained by medium composition if the experiment used rich medium, a group from GPR connective errors, and a residue from the growth threshold.

Then do the sensitivity: rerun at thresholds of 1%, 5% and 10% of wild-type growth and tabulate how precision and recall move. If a tenth of your false positives disappear at a looser threshold, the disagreement was partly definitional rather than biological, and saying so is more honest than quoting one F1 score.

Finally, estimate how many disagreements are data errors rather than model errors — experimental essentiality calls are themselves threshold-dependent and condition-specific. A defensible answer here is worth more than a high F1.

Assumptions behind this calculation

  • the experimental essentiality set is taken as the reference
  • a single medium and condition
  • essentiality treated as binary rather than graded

Common misconceptions

  • That a confusion matrix is the result. The gene-by-gene classification of every false prediction is the result; the matrix is the summary.
  • That essentiality is binary. Both the prediction and the experiment apply a growth threshold, and genes near it are called differently by different studies.
  • That a false positive is a model failure. Most are missing knowledge — an isozyme, a transporter, a medium component — which is a curation task rather than a method defect.

Check yourself before moving on

Q. A screen gives precision 0.70 and recall 0.81. Which error class dominates, and what does it say about the reconstruction?

A. Precision below recall means false positives outnumber false negatives: the model calls genes essential that experimentally are not. That says the reconstruction knows fewer alternative routes than the cell actually has — missing isozymes, unannotated promiscuous activities, absent transporters, medium components not in the modelled medium, or GPR rules written as AND where the biology is OR. It is a knowledge gap rather than a method failure, and the remedy is curation.

Q. Give three mechanisms that produce a false negative, and say why they are more interesting than false positives.

A. Regulation the model does not represent, so a stoichiometrically available route is not expressed; accumulation of a toxic intermediate when the usual route is blocked, which stoichiometry cannot see; and moonlighting or non-metabolic essentiality, such as a role in division or envelope integrity. They are more interesting because they point at the model's structural limits rather than at missing biochemical knowledge: no amount of curation fixes them, and each identifies a class of question constraint-based models cannot answer.

Q. Explain a phenotypic phase plane's regions from stoichiometry rather than from the plot.

A. At high oxygen relative to carbon, the cell can fully oxidise the substrate and growth is carbon-limited, so the objective rises with carbon uptake and is insensitive to oxygen. At low oxygen relative to carbon, respiration cannot reoxidise all the NADH produced, so fermentation products are secreted and growth is oxygen-limited, rising with oxygen and insensitive to carbon. Between them lies the line of optimality, where both are used in the ratio that the stoichiometry makes most efficient. Each region boundary is a point where the binding constraint changes, which is why the plane is piecewise linear.
