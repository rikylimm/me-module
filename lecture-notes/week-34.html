Week 34 of 55 · The molecular toolbox · Core

Tuning expression: promoters, RBS, copy number and burden

Week content

Pathway flux is set less by which genes you add than by how much of each enzyme you make, and week 31 already said the optimum allocates protein so that control coefficients match protein shares. This week turns that into construction: how far each dial really moves expression, how large the resulting library is, whether the screen can cover it, and how to measure the cost of the extra protein without fooling yourself with the wrong control.

Core ideas

1. The dials are unequal, and they multiply

Between a gene and a working catalyst sit copy number (roughly 1–50× across usable origins), promoter strength (about three orders of magnitude across a characterised library), translation initiation set by the ribosome binding site and 5′ UTR structure (two to three orders), codon usage (a few fold), and folding and proteolysis (0–100% of what was translated). The ranges multiply, so promoter and ribosome binding site together already cover more range than any pathway needs: the problem is resolution within it, not reach. Resolution is where prediction runs out — initiation depends on Shine-Dalgarno spacing, 5′ UTR folding energy and the gene’s first codons, which fold into the same structure, so calculators rank variants usefully but predict absolute rate only to about an order of magnitude. They build a spread to measure rather than specify a level.

2. Architecture carries information the parts list does not

In an operon, position matters: transcripts are degraded from the 5′ end and polymerase falls off, so downstream genes are expressed less and gene order is itself a dial. Insulating with a ribozyme or strong terminator removes the coupling, and terminators are graded rather than binary, so read-through into a downstream module is a common reason a part behaves differently in context. Lee §13.5 engineers this layer explicitly: about 12,000 synthetic expression modules combining the UP element, the −35/−10 spacing, the transcription start site and the Shine-Dalgarno-to-start spacer, yielding variants up to 13-fold above an already strong housekeeping promoter.

3. Combinatorial design is arithmetic before it is biology

A library of m modules at v variants has vᵐ members and needs more clones than members: a given variant is absent from N random picks with probability about e^(−N/L), so N = 3L covers 95% of variants in expectation and N = L covers 63%. Four modules at five variants is 625 members and 1,875 clones — past a hand-picked microtitre screen and inside a plate-based colorimetric one. Published practice agrees: Ajikumar's taxadiene work split the pathway into two modules at the IPP node and screened 16 strains, precisely because no high-throughput taxadiene screen existed.

4. The screen, not the library, sets the ceiling

Order-of-magnitude throughputs: hand-picked microtitre cultures 10²–10³ a week; plate colour or halo screens 10⁴–10⁵; flow cytometry on a fluorescent proxy 10⁷–10⁸; droplet microfluidics 10⁶–10⁸; mass-spectrometry screening 10³–10⁴, with the advantage of measuring the actual product. The proxy is the catch: fluorescence is not titre. Match format to library size, then ask what the readout omits and design the confirmation assay for the winners.

5. Burden has four mechanisms and they are distinguishable

Ribosome competition from heterologous translation; precursor and cofactor drain; folding and chaperone load, including inclusion bodies; and the replication cost of the plasmid. They respond to different interventions, so a titre that falls with the strongest promoter diagnoses nothing until they are separated — by growth rate, a ribosome-fraction reporter, labelled-precursor fate and an inert-protein comparison. The strongest promoter often lowers titre because two of these rise faster than flux does, and because the amplified step's control coefficient falls as it saturates.

6. The burden control is inert protein at matched mass, not an empty vector

An empty vector measures replication cost alone and so charges all synthesis cost to the pathway. The comparison is an equivalent mass of a protein that does nothing, from the same promoter, ribosome binding site and plasmid, with the mass verified rather than inferred from the promoter's reputation. Pathway strain minus that control is the metabolic drain; control minus plasmid-free strain is the synthesis and replication cost.

Worked anchor

Size a tuning library to a real screen, then price its burden.

Four modules — precursor supply, the committed step, the downstream branch, cofactor regeneration — at five promoter-RBS variants each: L = 5⁴ = 625, and 95% expected coverage needs N = 3L = 1,875 clones.

The screen: the product is colourless with no sensor yet, so detection is LC-MS on supernatant. At 6 minutes a sample, 20 hours a day, that is 1,000 a working week. Covering 1,875 takes two weeks with no room for repeats; a one-week budget allows about 1,000 picks, N/L = 1.6, which finds 1 − e^(−1.6) = 80% of variants.

Redesign. Fix cofactor regeneration at the level week 31's allocation analysis implies and drop to three modules at four variants: L = 4³ = 64, N = 192 clones. One day of instrument time, room for triplicate controls, and the winner can be re-screened rather than believed once.

Which module gets the widest variation comes from the control coefficients: in the week 31 model the committed step carried C = 0.58 against a protein share of 0.21, the largest gap in the set.

The burden. Say the winner expresses the pathway at 15% of total protein against a metabolic sector of about 40%. If catalytic capacity scales with allocated protein and the drain comes from that sector, remaining capacity is (40 − 15)/40 = 0.63, which at a wild-type 0.60 h⁻¹ predicts about 0.38 h⁻¹: a 37% growth penalty, and the dominant cost where productivity is biomass times specific rate. Treat it as a bound, and test it with the inert-protein control at the same 15%.

Assumptions behind this calculation

  • library members equiprobable and picks independent, so coverage is 1 − e^(−N/L)
  • one LC-MS instrument at 6 minutes per sample, 20 h a day
  • pathway at 15% of total protein and the metabolic sector at 40%
  • catalytic capacity proportional to the protein allocated to it
  • the whole pathway drain taken from the metabolic sector
  • control coefficients and protein shares carried over from the week 31 model

Common misconceptions

  • That the strongest promoter gives the most product. Above the allocation optimum the extra protein buys saturated enzyme at rising burden, and the amplified step's control coefficient falls as its substrate is drawn down.
  • That a ribosome binding site calculator sets the expression level. It ranks variants and predicts absolute rate to about an order of magnitude; it is a tool for building a spread that is then measured.
  • That an empty-vector control measures burden. It measures replication cost. The control is an equivalent, verified mass of inert protein from the same construct.

Check yourself before moving on

Q. Why does the strongest promoter often lower titre, and which four mechanisms would you distinguish experimentally?

A. Because flux stops responding while cost keeps rising: the enzyme saturates, its control coefficient falls, and the protein comes from a fixed budget. The four are ribosome competition, precursor or cofactor drain, folding load and plasmid replication cost. Separate them with inert protein at matched verified mass from the same construct, a ribosome reporter, labelled precursor fate into product against biomass, and the soluble-versus-inclusion-body split; a plasmid-free strain isolates replication.

Q. Your library is 625 members and your screen does 1,000 assays a week. What do you do?

A. Recognise 1,000 picks as 1.6-fold coverage, finding 80% of variants with no replicates. Either enlarge the screen — a fluorescent or growth-coupled proxy moves throughput by orders of magnitude, at the price of measuring a proxy — or shrink the library, usually the right answer: fix the modules allocation analysis says are near optimum, keep three at four variants for 64 members and 192 picks, and spend the recovered capacity on replicates.

Q. Which module should carry the widest variation, and how do you decide before building anything?

A. The one whose flux control coefficient most exceeds its share of the pathway protein budget, since the two are equal at the optimum and a gap marks mis-allocation. Compute both from the week 31 model and the proteomics: control above share means amplification should pay, below means protein can be given up. Decide this first, because vᵐ makes every extra varied module expensive.
