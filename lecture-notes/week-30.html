Kinetic models of metabolism

Week content

Where dynamics returns. A kinetic model answers questions no stoichiometric method can touch — concentrations, behaviour away from steady state, which enzyme controls the flux — at the price of parameters nobody can measure in vivo. The craft is in handling that price: thermodynamic consistency imposed as a constraint, parameters carried as ranges, and an ensemble reported instead of one fit with a false precision.

Core ideas

1. What a kinetic model buys, and when it is the wrong tool

Four things FBA cannot give: metabolite concentrations, time-dependent behaviour, the response to a change in enzyme level, and control coefficients. The cost is a rate law and several parameters per reaction, and the parameters are the problem — in vitro kcat and Kₘ come from dilute buffer at saturating substrate, while the cell is crowded, at a different pH, and working near Kₘ by design. The decision rule is scope: model the sub-pathway you have a specific dynamic question about, ten to twenty reactions anchored by measured concentrations, and use FBA for anything genome-scale. Whole-cell kinetic modelling is usually the wrong first move.

2. The reversible rate law and the Haldane relation

For one substrate and one product, v = (V_f·S/K_S − V_r·P/K_P)/(1 + S/K_S + P/K_P). Four parameters, but not four free ones: at equilibrium v = 0, giving K_eq = (V_f·K_P)/(V_r·K_S). That is the Haldane relation, and since K_eq is fixed by ΔrG′° only three of the four may be fitted. Impose it as a constraint rather than checking afterwards. A fit with all four free will return a parameter set implying an equilibrium constant that contradicts the thermodynamics, and such a model reproduces every data point while being unphysical — the worst failure mode available, because it looks like success.

3. Approximative rate laws, and when each is honest

Where the mechanism is unknown, a generic form is more honest than a mechanistic one invented to look rigorous. Convenience kinetics generalises the reversible Michaelis-Menten form to any stoichiometry, is thermodynamically consistent by construction, and needs one Kₘ per reactant plus a turnover number. Lin-log writes the rate as linear in log concentrations about a reference state, which nearly linearises the steady-state problem: excellent for control analysis near a known operating point, poor far from it. Power laws give the S-system machinery with the same local caveat. The criterion is not sophistication — mechanistic where the mechanism is established and the parameters measured, generic and thermodynamically consistent everywhere else.

4. Where the parameters come from, and what to do with the spread

BRENDA for kcat and Kₘ, read as a distribution rather than a value: entries for one enzyme commonly span an order of magnitude, reflecting real differences in assay conditions, isoforms and preparation purity rather than sloppiness. Not the mean, which corresponds to no experiment; not the most recent, since recency is not accuracy. Carry the range through and test whether the conclusion survives it — if it does not, the study's output is that the parameter must be measured, which is a legitimate result. Thermodynamics removes one parameter per reaction through the Haldane relation; the rest are fitted to the measured steady state, usually leaving the problem heavily underdetermined. Be clear about what the fitted values then are. The cytoplasm holds 300–400 g L⁻¹ of macromolecules occupying over 30% of cell volume, so excluded-volume effects raise thermodynamic activities, slow diffusion and shift apparent binding affinities: an in vivo Kₘ is an effective parameter for a crowded, non-specifically binding medium, not the buffer value. Expect to fit parameters that differ from BRENDA's by more than the BRENDA spread, and say so rather than calling the discrepancy a fitting artefact.

5. Stiffness, and verifying the steady state independently

Pools turn over in seconds while the culture changes over hours, so the eigenvalues span orders of magnitude and the ODEs are stiff. An explicit solver will crawl or go unstable; use BDF, Radau or LSODA and set tolerances deliberately rather than taking defaults. Then verify without the integrator: solve S·v(c) = 0 with a root finder from the integrator's endpoint and confirm the two agree. This catches the two commonest silent failures — a solver stopped on a slowly drifting trajectory and called it steady, and a conservation relation that makes the Jacobian singular and the steady state non-unique.

6. Sloppiness, and ensembles instead of best fits

The Hessian of the objective at the fit has an eigenvalue spectrum spanning six or more decades: a few stiff directions in which the output is highly sensitive, many sloppy ones in which parameters move by large factors with no measurable effect. So individual parameters are unidentifiable even when the model predicts well and a fitted Kₘ must not be read mechanistically; predictions along stiff directions can be trusted further than the parameters behind them; and design should target the stiff combinations, since constraining a sloppy direction buys nothing. The practical response is to stop reporting one parameter set — sample a hundred consistent with the data and the thermodynamics, and propagate the ensemble to a prediction interval.

Worked anchor

Imposing the Haldane relation, and what happens when you do not.

Take a reaction with ΔrG′° = −7.5 kJ mol⁻¹ at 37 °C, where RT = 2.577 kJ mol⁻¹. Then K_eq = exp(7.5/2.577) = exp(2.910) = 18.4.

With V_f = 10 mmol gDW⁻¹ h⁻¹, K_S = 0.2 mM and K_P = 0.5 mM from BRENDA, the reverse maximal rate is not free:

V_r = V_f·K_P/(K_S·K_eq) = 10 × 0.5/(0.2 × 18.4) = 5/3.68 = 1.36 mmol gDW⁻¹ h⁻¹.

Now suppose all four had been left free and the fit returned V_r = 6. Read the implied equilibrium constant back out:

K_eq = V_f·K_P/(V_r·K_S) = (10 × 0.5)/(6 × 0.2) = 4.17,

so the implied ΔrG′° = −2.577 × ln(4.17) = −3.68 kJ mol⁻¹. The fit has silently asserted a standard free energy 3.8 kJ mol⁻¹ from the measured one.

Be careful how much that proves. A group-contribution estimate carries 3–4 kJ mol⁻¹ of uncertainty, as week 27 established, so against an estimated value this discrepancy sits inside the noise. The argument for the constraint is structural rather than numerical: unconstrained, nothing stops the fit drifting much further, and the drift is invisible because the model still reproduces the data. Where ΔrG′° is measured rather than estimated — and for much of central metabolism it is — a discrepancy this size is a genuine contradiction and the fit must be rejected.

What the constraint does is worth seeing. Before: four free parameters per reaction, twenty for a five-step pathway, against perhaps six measured concentrations and one flux — hopelessly underdetermined. After: fifteen, still underdetermined, but every member of the solution set is at least thermodynamically possible. The Haldane relation does not identify your parameters; it removes a family of unphysical fits that would have passed every check you run.

So make the underdetermination visible. Refit from a hundred random starts, keep every set that reproduces the data, confirm the sensitivity spectrum spans decades, then propagate the ensemble through the prediction you care about — the gain from doubling the first enzyme — and set the interval beside the best fit's point estimate. That gap is this week's result and Brief 10's closing requirement.

Assumptions behind this calculation

  • one substrate and one product, so the four-parameter form applies
  • RT = 2.577 kJ mol⁻¹ at 37 °C
  • BRENDA values taken to apply in vivo, which §5.3 says they do not
  • ΔrG′° read at a stated pH, pMg and ionic strength

Common misconceptions

  • That more data fixes unidentifiability. Sloppiness is structural: only measurements constraining the stiff combinations help, which is why the spectrum should be computed before the next experiment is designed.
  • That a fitted Kₘ can be interpreted mechanistically. Not unless it lies along a stiff direction, and most do not. Fitted parameters are coordinates in a large flat region, not estimates of physical quantities.
  • That thermodynamic consistency is a finishing check. Imposed afterwards it rejects a fit you have published a figure from; imposed as a constraint it removes the unphysical region before the optimiser enters it.

Check yourself before moving on

Q. Five BRENDA entries give kcat spanning an order of magnitude. What do you do, and why not the obvious alternatives?

A. Carry the range through and test whether the conclusion survives it. Not the mean, because the spread is not noise about a true value — it reflects different assay conditions, isoforms and preparations, so the mean corresponds to no experiment. Not the most recent, because recency is not accuracy. Not the highest on the argument that the others contained inactive enzyme, which is a story rather than evidence. If the conclusion holds across the range you have a robust result; if it flips, the study's output is that the parameter must be measured for your organism and condition, which is the finding that justifies the next experiment.

Q. Your integrator reports a steady state. How do you verify it, and what two failures are you looking for?

A. Verify without the integrator: take its endpoint as a starting guess, solve S·v(c) = 0 with a root finder, and confirm the concentration vectors agree with a residual genuinely near zero rather than merely small relative to the fluxes. The first failure this catches is a solver stopped on a slowly drifting trajectory, since in a stiff system a slow mode looks flat over the integration window while still moving, making the steady state depend on how long you ran. The second is a singular Jacobian from a conservation relation: if total adenylate is conserved the steady state is fixed by the initial condition rather than unique, and the root finder says so by failing to converge.

Q. Why is a single best-fit parameter set misleading, and what should be reported instead?

A. Because these models are sloppy: output is highly sensitive to a few parameter combinations and nearly insensitive to many others, so individual parameters are unidentifiable from the available data even when the model predicts well. A single fit implies a precision that does not exist and invites mechanistic interpretation of numbers arbitrary within their unidentified directions. Report an ensemble consistent with the data and the thermodynamic constraints, propagated to a prediction interval for whatever the model is used to decide, with the sensitivity spectrum showing which combinations are determined — then target the next experiment at the stiff combinations rather than the least known parameter.
