Study protocol · five minutes

Teach it back

Recognition is not recall. Each week ends with a closed-book explanation, one calculation, and one boundary. Do it before you reread.

Four moves

  1. Explain from memory. In three to five minutes, state the question this week's concept answers, the mechanism or reasoning, and why it matters for metabolic engineering.
  2. Work a number. Redo the week's worked anchor on a blank page with units, then change one input or assumption and calculate the consequence. If the anchor is not quantitative, make a small numerical example that uses the week's concept.
  3. Name the boundary. State one important assumption, what evidence would support it, and one result that would make you revise your explanation.
  4. Log it. Record the explanation, calculation, and boundary in the tracker's week notes. Revisit any part you could only recognize while reading.

What good looks like

  • The explanation names a mechanism, not a definition.
  • The calculation has units, a stated interpretation, and one changed input.
  • The boundary is specific enough that a measurement could test it.
  • You can say what you do not know without filling the gap with a guess.

Use it with the case study

Apply the same four moves to the Fercaf thesis: separate what was measured from what was inferred, resolve the ratio notation, and audit the whiteness conclusion against SNI 7622:2011. Open the case study.
